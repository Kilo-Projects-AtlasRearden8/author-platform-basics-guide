# 2. Press release

A press release tells journalists, bloggers, librarians, and bookstores that your book exists. It is free to write and free to send. You do not need a PR firm.

## What goes in a press release

Standard order, top to bottom:

1. **FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE** — or a date if you are timing it
2. **Headline** — one line, what happened
3. **Sub-headline** — one line, why it matters
4. **Dateline** — city, state, date
5. **Lead paragraph** — who, what, when, where, why in 2–3 sentences
6. **Body** — 2–4 short paragraphs: the book, the author, why readers care, any local angle
7. **Quote** — from you, in your own voice
8. **Boilerplate** — short about-the-author / about-the-publisher paragraph
9. **Contact** — name, author email, website

## Step by step

### Step 1: Pick your news hook
"Book released" is enough, but a hook helps:
- Local author publishes first book
- Nurse-turned-author explains AI in plain English
- Free guide helps small businesses use AI

### Step 2: Write the headline
Keep it under 12 words. Example:

> Indiana Nurse Publishes Beginner's Guide to AI: 'The Massive AI Manual'

### Step 3: Fill the template

> FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
>
> **[Headline]**
>
> **[Sub-headline]**
>
> **[City], [State]** — **[Month Day, Year]** — **[Author name]** today announced the release of **[book title]**, a **[genre/category]** book published by **[publisher or "self-published"]**. [One sentence describing the book and who it is for.]
>
> [Second sentence: the problem the book solves and why it matters now.]
>
> **[Author name]** is a **[credential/role]** based in **[state]**. [One sentence of background.]
>
> "I wrote this book because..." — **[Author name]**
>
> **[Book title]** is available at **[retailers]**. [Format and price if you want to share.]
>
> About **[Author name]** / Asdale Dynamics, LLC:
> [Boilerplate: 2–3 sentences about who you are and what you publish.]
>
> ###
>
> Media contact:
> **[Author name]**
> **[Author email]**
> **[Website]**

## Where to send it (free)

- Your local newspaper (city desk or community editor)
- Local radio stations and community calendars
- Library newsletters and bookstore event coordinators
- Bloggers and newsletter writers in your topic area (find them via Google, not purchased lists)
- Free release distribution services (check their free tiers carefully; read privacy terms before signing up)

## Privacy-safe checklist

- [ ] Use an author email, not a personal one
- [ ] No home address — city and state only in the dateline
- [ ] No personal phone number unless you want calls there
- [ ] Do not send to purchased media lists
