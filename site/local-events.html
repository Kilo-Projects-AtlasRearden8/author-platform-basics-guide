7. Local events

Local events put you in front of readers in your community: libraries, independent bookstores, community centers, and festivals. Most are free to attend or host.

Step 1: Choose your event type

TypeBest forTypical cost
Library talk or readingNon-fiction, how-to, local interestFree
Bookstore signingFiction, local authorsFree (often consignment stock)
Community-center workshopPractical topics, AI guides, budgetingFree or room fee
Virtual event (library or Zoom)Wider reach, low energyFree

Start with a library talk — libraries love local authors and programming is usually free.

Step 2: Contact the venue

Email or call 6–8 weeks ahead. Keep it short:

Template — copy and fill in the bracketed parts.
Subject: Event proposal: [Book title] talk at [venue] Hi [Venue contact name], I'm [Author name], a [role/credential] based in [city/state] and author of [Book title], a [genre] book about [topic]. [One sentence of background.] I'd like to propose a [30–60 minute] talk/reading at [venue] for [audience], ideally [month/season]. I can bring copies of my book to sign or sell, and I'll handle my own promotion. Are you scheduling events for [season]? I'd be happy to answer any questions. Thanks, [Author name] [Author email] [Website]

Step 3: Plan the event

Simple structure for a 45-minute slot:

  • 5 minutes: welcome and introductions
  • 10 minutes: who you are and why you wrote the book
  • 15 minutes: reading or demo (one practical thing the audience can try)
  • 10 minutes: Q&A
  • 5 minutes: where to find the book + newsletter sign-up

Bring:

  • Copies of your book (if selling, check venue rules on sales)
  • A sign-up sheet: name and email only, with a one-line privacy note ("I'll use this to send my occasional newsletter. You can opt out anytime.")
  • Business cards or flyers with your website and newsletter link

Step 4: Promote locally (free)

  • Send your press release (Chapter 2) to the local paper and community calendar.
  • Ask the venue to list it in their newsletter and on their events page.
  • Post on your socials and local community groups (Facebook neighborhood groups, library pages).
  • Put a flyer on community bulletin boards (library, coffee shop) — check first.

Step 5: After the event

  • Add sign-up sheet emails to your newsletter list only if the sheet said you would.
  • Send a thank-you email to the venue.
  • Post a thank-you on social (no photos of attendees without permission).

Virtual events

Same structure over Zoom, Google Meet, or the library's platform. Send the link to registrants only, use a waiting room, and do not publish the link publicly if you want to keep the event small.

Privacy-safe checklist

  • Sign-up sheet collects name and email only
  • Privacy note on the sheet explains how emails will be used
  • No photos of attendees without permission
  • No home address on public flyers — use the venue address
  • Use an author email for event contact
