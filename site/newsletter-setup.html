3. Newsletter setup

A newsletter is the most valuable part of an author platform: it is a direct line to readers who asked to hear from you. Social platforms can change their rules; your email list is yours.

You can start for free. This chapter uses free-tier tools and privacy-safe habits throughout.

Step 1: Pick a free tool

ToolFree tier (check current limits)Notes
SubstackFree to start; cut only if you charge paid subscriptionsSimple, no design skills needed
BeehiivFree up to ~2,500 subscribersGood analytics, referral program
MailerLiteFree up to 1,000 subscribers / 12,000 emails/monthAutomation and landing pages included

Pick one and stay with it until you outgrow it.

Step 2: Create your account (privacy-safe)

  • Use your author email address, not your personal one.
  • Use a strong, unique password and save it in your password manager.
  • Do not enter your home address unless the service legally requires a postal address in email footers. If required, you can often use a PO box or business address instead.
  • Read the privacy policy before you import anything. Never import a list you did not collect yourself.

Step 3: Set up your landing page

A landing page is where people sign up. Keep it tiny:

  • Headline: what readers get ("New book releases, writing updates, and free AI guides — once a month")
  • One form field: email address
  • Optional second field: first name (only if you will actually use it)
  • A one-sentence privacy note ("We never sell or share your email. You can unsubscribe anytime.")

Step 4: Write your welcome email

The welcome email is sent right after someone confirms. It sets expectations.

Template — copy and fill in the bracketed parts.
Subject: Welcome + [free gift, if you have one] Hi [First name], thanks for signing up. I'm [Author name], author of [Book title]. Here is what to expect: [frequency] emails about [topics]. You can unsubscribe at any time — the link is at the bottom of every email. [If you have a free gift:] As promised, here is [gift link]. [If you have a book out:] You can find [Book title] at [retailer links]. Questions? Just reply to this email. I read every reply. Best, [Author name]

Step 5: Collect subscribers without ads

  • Put the landing page link in your social bios, book back matter, and email signature.
  • Mention the newsletter at local events (with a sign-up sheet that says how you will use emails).
  • Offer a reader magnet only if you already have something to give (a free chapter, a checklist, a reading guide). It is optional.

Never buy a list. Never add people because they bought your book or followed you on social.

Step 6: Send on a cadence you can keep

Once a month is plenty to start. Write in a plain, warm voice — like a friend would. If you miss a month, just send the next one; no apologies needed.

Privacy-safe checklist

  • Double opt-in is turned on (subscribers confirm their email)
  • Every email has a working unsubscribe link
  • You only email people who opted in
  • No home address or personal phone in public footers unless legally required (use a PO box/business address instead)
  • You never sell or share the list
